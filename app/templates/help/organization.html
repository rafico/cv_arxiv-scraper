Collections

Group papers into named folders such as “Thesis Ch. 3”, “Survey candidates”, or “Lab Meeting”. A paper can belong to more than one collection.

Create a collection

Click + next to Collections in the sidebar to create a named folder for a project, reading list, or meeting. With a collection open, the sidebar can Rename or Delete it; deleting a collection keeps its papers in your library.

Add papers

Click the folder icon on any paper card to file it into one or more collections, or pick + New collection… there to start one with that paper. To file many at once, click Select, tick the papers, and use Add to collection in the bar that appears. Inside a collection, the same folder menu has Remove from collection.

Grow a collection

With a collection open, the sidebar offers Suggest similar — papers semantically close to what’s already in it, one click to add. Prior works lists papers outside your library that two or more members cite, taken from their stored Semantic Scholar reference lists (filled by the daily scrape or cv-arxiv-backfill citation-edges; OpenAlex-only references don’t count) and looked up live on Semantic Scholar — a Semantic Scholar API key avoids throttling. Papers with an arXiv id get a one-click Add. To seed it with known papers, including ones older than your feed, paste arXiv ids, arXiv links or a .bib into Add by arXiv id / URL / .bib (up to 100 at a time). Papers are fetched from arXiv without counting as saves, and .bib entries without an arXiv id are skipped. To pull in a whole topic, cv-arxiv-sync --query imports an arXiv search into a collection (see CLI).

Export or share it

The same sidebar has Export .bib (a ready bibliography of just this collection), Download CSV (a screening spreadsheet), and Share (a portable JSON bundle a colleague can import). The Graph page can also be filtered to one collection.

Use collections when you want a paper to live inside a specific project or bucket.

Tags

Add custom tags with the + tag button. Tags are best for cross-cutting labels like “methods”, “datasets”, or “to-cite” that may apply across many collections.

Use tags when you want lightweight labels that cut across projects, not separate folders.

Notes

Each paper has a free-text notes field under More details. Use it for takeaways, quotes, open questions, or reminders. Notes auto-save as you type.

Reading Status

Track where each paper is in your reading pipeline.

To Read Reading Read

Set the status from the dropdown on each paper card, then filter by reading status in Advanced Filters when you want a reading queue.

Follow & Mute

Follow an author

Expand a paper’s details and click Follow. The first author is added to your tracked authors list so future papers from them rank higher.

Mute a topic

Click Mute when a topic keeps producing noise. Future papers with that topic tag are pushed down or suppressed.
